08 — Normality Limit Optimizer

Finds the tightest symmetric USL/LSL (centred on μ) such that the data subset within those limits passes the Anderson-Darling normality test. Two targets are computed: p > 0.05 (5% significance) and p > 0.10 (10% significance — stricter normality requirement). A minimum of 20 observations must remain in the subset.

09 — Bayesian Specification Limit Estimator

Uses a Normal-Inverse-Gamma conjugate prior over (μ, σ²) to compute the posterior predictive distribution for a single future observation, then derives credible-interval–based spec limits. Prior strength (κ₀) controls how much weight is given to prior beliefs vs the data. Results include posterior μ, σ, and both equal-tails and HDI spec limits at your chosen coverage.

Prior strength κ₀=1 ≈ weak prior · κ₀=0.01 ≈ near-flat (data-dominated) · κ₀=10 ≈ strong prior

10 — Quality Loss Function & Optimal Target

Computes the Taguchi Quality Loss Function L(y) = k(y−T)², where T is the nominal target and k = A₀/Δ² is the loss coefficient. The optimal target minimises expected loss E[L(y)] = k(σ² + (μ−T)²). Three loss types: Nominal-is-Best, Smaller-is-Better, Larger-is-Better.

Δ=0 uses half the current spec width automatically

11 — CQT Asymmetric Loss Capability Index

Based on Tahir, Yadav & Singh (2026) — extends Cpm by replacing the symmetric squared-error penalty with an asymmetric quadratic-exponential loss (QTSELF): LQT(τ,T) = (τ−T)²·ec(τ−T). When c=0, CQT = Cpm exactly. Positive c penalises overestimation; negative c penalises underestimation.

Classical Cpm (Taguchi — symmetric, c=0)
Cpm = (USL−LSL) / 6√(σ² + (μ−T)²)
Loss in denominator: (μ−T)² — equal penalty for over- and under-estimation. Reduces to Cp when μ=T.
Proposed CQT (asymmetric, c≠0)
CQT = (USL−LSL) / 6√(σ² + (μ−T)²·ec(μ−T))
When μ>T (overestimation) and c>0: exponential term >1, denominator grows, CQT < Cpm — correctly penalising the over-shoot.
